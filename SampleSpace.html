Probability theory project

What happens when you flip a coin 100,000 times?

You can't predict a single coin flip. But flip a coin 100,000 times and heads will come up almost exactly half the time. This site is about why that happens. First you list everything that can happen, then you run simulations with a coin and a die and compare the results with the math.

What's on the site

Two ideas to start with

Sample spaceThe list of everything that can happen. For a coin it's {H, T}, for a die {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}. A probability is the number of outcomes you want divided by the number of all outcomes.
Law of large numbersRepeat an experiment many times and the average gets close to the theoretical value: 0.5 for a coin, 3.5 for a die. The typical error is about σ/√n.
Reference numbersCoin: mean 0.5, σ = 0.5. Die: mean 3.5, variance 35/12 ≈ 2.917, σ ≈ 1.708.

Step 1 of 5

Sample space

To say how likely something is, you first need to know what can happen at all. That list is called the sample space. Once you have it, finding a probability comes down to counting.

Experiment
Anything where you can't know the result ahead of time: flipping a coin, rolling a die, drawing a card without looking.
Outcome
One possible result. Rolling a 4 is one outcome.
Sample space S
The list of all possible outcomes, written in curly brackets. Coin: S = {H, T}. Die: S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}.
Event
The outcomes you care about. "Roll an even number" is the event A = {2, 4, 6}. An event is always part of S.

How to find a probability

If every outcome is equally likely, as with a fair coin or a fair die, the probability of an event A is:

P(A) = number of outcomes in Anumber of outcomes in S

Example: on a die, A = "even number" = {2, 4, 6}. A has 3 outcomes and S has 6, so P(A) = 3/6 = 1/2. You get an even number half the time.

A few rules always hold:

  • A probability is between 0 and 1. P = 0 means it can't happen (a 7 on one die). P = 1 means it always happens (a number from 1 to 6).
  • The probabilities of all outcomes in S add up to 1. Each face of a die has 1/6, and 6 × 1/6 = 1.
  • The chance that A doesn't happen is 1 − P(A). Not rolling a 6 is 1 − 1/6 = 5/6.

More than one coin or die

To count the outcomes, multiply. A coin has 2 options and a die has 6, so you multiply the numbers for each one. This is called the multiplication rule.

Two coins
2 × 2 = 4 outcomes
S = {HH, HT, TH, TT}
A coin and a die
2 × 6 = 12 outcomes
S = {(H, 1), (H, 2), …, (T, 6)}
Two dice
6 × 6 = 36 outcomes
S = {(1, 1), (1, 2), …, (6, 6)}

People often say two coins have three outcomes: two heads, two tails, or one of each. But "one of each" can happen in two ways. The first coin is heads and the second is tails (HT), or the other way round (TH). It helps to picture one gold coin and one silver coin.

So P(one of each) = 2/4 = 1/2, not 1/3. You can check this in the calculator below: pick 2 coins, "exactly" 1 head, and run 10,000 trials.

One odd thing: the die average at n = 10,000 (3.4996) is closer to 3.5 than the one at n = 100,000 (3.4948). That's just chance. The law says the error shrinks on average, not every single time.

  • Die faces: χ² = 2.14, p = 0.83. Nothing suggests the simulated die is unfair.
  • Heads in 20 flips against Bin(20, 0.5): χ² = 20.7 with 14 degrees of freedom, p = 0.11. It fits.
  • A coin with p = 0.55: the test missed it with 100 flips (p = 0.11) and caught it with 1,000 (p = 0.011).

Step 5 of 5

What I found

What happened

The coin and the die behaved the way the theory says. After 100,000 flips the share of heads was 0.50089. The error shrinks like 1/√n, so to get ten times more accurate you need a hundred times more flips.

Where the targets come from

Both targets come from the sample space. Heads is 1 outcome out of 2, so the share heads toward 1/2. Each die face is 1 out of 6, and the average of 1 to 6 is 21/6 = 3.5.

Limits

Computer random numbers are pseudo-random, so this only imitates a real coin. One run proves little, so it's worth repeating the experiments. The fairness test also needs enough data: with 100 flips it missed a coin that lands heads 55% of the time.
